Knitwear & Garment Care

The Cashmere Guide: Sourcing, Combing, and Proper Garment Care

How to distinguish high-grade Mongolian wool and keep your knitwear soft and pilling-free for decades.

Published by Willow & Heaven Archive · Original Style Essay

Few materials capture the idea of quiet luxury quite like cashmere. Its softness gives even a simple knit an inviting character, while its lightweight warmth makes it particularly useful when building layered outfits.

But the appeal of cashmere is not only about softness. A well-made garment depends on the quality of its fibers, the way they are spun and knitted, and the care given to the finished piece. Understanding those details can make choosing and maintaining knitwear much easier.

The best knitwear rewards attention: the better it is cared for, the longer it can remain part of the wardrobe.

Start With the Fiber

Cashmere is valued for its fine fibers and soft hand. Origin, fiber characteristics, processing, and construction can all influence the final garment. Instead of relying only on a label or a luxurious description, it is useful to consider the material and construction as a whole.

The density of a knit can also change its feel. Some pieces are designed to be light and delicate, while others have greater substance. The right choice depends on whether the garment is intended to sit close to the body or become part of a layered winter wardrobe.

Why Pilling Happens

Small pills can appear when loose fibers become tangled on the surface of a knitted garment. Friction from movement, bags, coats, and other layers can contribute to this process.

Pilling does not automatically mean that a sweater is poorly made. It can occur with natural fibers, particularly in areas that experience repeated contact. The important part is learning how to manage it without damaging the knit.

Gentle Maintenance Matters

Cashmere generally benefits from careful handling. Following the garment's specific care instructions is the safest starting point, particularly because different constructions may have different requirements.

When surface pills appear, a suitable garment comb or other gentle method can help remove loose fibers. The process should be controlled rather than aggressive, since pulling or scraping too forcefully can disturb the knitted structure.

Store Knitwear With Care

Storage is another important part of garment longevity. Folded knitwear is generally easier to protect from unnecessary stretching than pieces left hanging for long periods. Keeping garments clean and properly stored also helps them remain ready for future seasons.

Giving knitwear enough space can also help preserve its shape. Instead of treating delicate pieces as ordinary basics, thinking of them as materials that require a little attention can significantly change how long they remain useful.

A good cashmere garment is ultimately less about novelty than continuity. When its fiber, construction, fit, and care come together, it can become one of those quiet wardrobe pieces that returns season after season. Its value grows not through constant replacement, but through familiarity and thoughtful use.
